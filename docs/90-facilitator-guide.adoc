== Delivery

Brad drives; Jon is the color commentator — interrupt, add commentary, call out
misspeaks live, and take over if the driver flounders. The number one goal is to
entertain: a tuned-out audience learns nothing. Keep it light, and if humour
lands on anyone, self-deprecate first.

Five planted questions are rehearsed; the console marks each one PLANTED. On
the data-to-judgement slide (5) Jon asks "so is AI taking our jobs?" — the
answer is that it is not, because it has no judgement. On the architecture
slide (9) Jon plays the foil with "what's a seam?" (the pants analogy) and Brad
asks Jon "what is fine-tuning to you?" — the studying-for-the-test answer. On
the why-one-platform slide (11) Jon challenges lock-in ("I thought we already
bought the software" — the phone-plan analogy), and on the adoption-path slide
(14) Jon asks what a predicament is versus a problem. Plan deliberate pauses to
engage the room.

The theme that holds the hour together is the ladder: data → information →
knowledge → judgement. Every slide shows the rungs it serves in the top-right
spine, and every technology, outcome and lab carries a rung chip in the same
four colours (blue, amber, red, green). Use it as the connective tissue: when a
product name lands on screen, say which rung it climbs and who climbs the
next one. Judgement is always a person; automation executes it.

The full talk track, including the seams, lock-in and MTTR riffs, lives in
`slides/presenter-notes.html` — open it beside the deck and advance both
together. Each console step lists the slide's rungs and an "On the ladder"
beat mapping every item on the slide to its rung.

== Which labs to run live

Lab 01:: Always. Step 4 — the endpoint bound to `127.0.0.1` — is the single
most persuasive screen in the whole deck for a tax agency audience.

Lab 05:: Almost always. Watching an alert resolve itself in 62 seconds lands
harder than any slide about automation.

Lab 06:: If security staff are in the room, run steps 3 to 5. The deliberate
`NON-COMPLIANT` result and the drift misreading are both good discussion.

Lab 04:: Run step 4 — the refusal — if anyone raises hallucination. It is a
30-second answer to a question that otherwise consumes ten minutes.

Lab 07:: If operations or SRE leaders are in the room. The cited diagnosis in
step 3 and the MTTR table in step 5 are the two screens that sell the
ops-notebook story.

Lab 08:: When the room wants the whole loop rather than one link of it. Steps
4, 6 and 7 (the score, the merged review, the 42-second remediation) carry the
argument; step 8, the assistant probing the estate, is the closer if time
allows.

Labs 02 and 03:: Usually better as a walkthrough than a live run. Both have
long-running steps that do not demo well in real time.

== Running the labs on a projector

[source,bash]
----
# Larger pauses, no colour issues on unfamiliar terminals
LAB_SPEED=0.08 LAB_PACE=step ./labs/lab01-rhel-ai/run.sh

# If the room's screen share mangles ANSI colour
NO_COLOR=1 LAB_PACE=step ./labs/lab01-rhel-ai/run.sh
----

Increase your terminal font size before the session, not during it. Eighty
columns is enough for every lab — none of the output wraps at that width.

If you would rather not type at all, open the interactive deck
(`slides/deck.html`) and switch to its *Demo* view — it plays every lab
hands-free with play/pause, per-lab jumps and a speed control, and each lab
terminal also has its own *Demo* button. Opening the page with `#demo` on the
URL starts the Demo view playing on a continuous loop, which doubles as a
holding screen before the session begins. For screen sharing, the *Present*
button (or kbd:[F]) fullscreens the 16:9 slide stage.

== The deck's switches

The interactive deck carries an *EN/ES* language tab in the sidebar footer —
Spanish audiences get the slides, lab briefings, theory panels and glossary
translated, while terminal commands and output stay English, as real tools
do. Next to it sit a light/dark *theme* toggle and the *Notes* button. Every
lab carries a theory panel ("what just happened"), and a glossary closes the
Labs view — when someone asks where an answer came from, the foundations
slides plus the Lab 01 theory panel are the prepared response.

As each command runs, a *step card* pops up under the terminal (beside it in
the Demo view) with three facets: what is happening, why it matters to this
talk, and the engineering seam a single Red Hat platform removes — the
hand-built box nobody documented, the glue script between alert and fix, the
scanner report nobody can diff. A wrap card closes each lab with what was
just proved. The cards are the talk track: read the middle facet aloud when
the room is mission-minded, the right-hand one when it is procurement-minded.
They follow the EN/ES tab like everything else.

Two more switches arrived after the September 15 review. The ladder now
sits in the corner of every slide as a small *stacked pyramid* (judgement at
the apex, data at the base, the rainbow's yellow and blue as thin bands
between); the rungs a slide serves are lit, the rest dimmed, so the room can
track where each slide lives on the climb without a legend. And the slides
that have an illustrated graphic (title, data to judgement, where AI lands,
five outcomes, architecture, why one platform, labs preview, next steps)
carry a small *graphic* pill; press `G`, the pill, or the Graphic button
under the stage to pop the picture out over the deck with three or four
discussion points beside it, then `Esc` or `G` again to close. The Labs view
opens the "Choose your path" map the same way. The pop-out shows the
illustrated render when the file is present in `slides/assets/graphics/`
and a vector rendition of the same content otherwise.

== Questions you will get, and short answers

"Can this run air-gapped?"::
Yes. Mirror images and models into the enclave with `oc-mirror`. The platform is
built for disconnected operation; it is a supported configuration, not a
workaround.

"What does it cost in GPUs?"::
An 8B model serving a pilot workload fits on one accelerator. Filing-season
concurrency is the number that sets the ceiling, and almost nobody knows theirs.
That measurement is a good first engagement.

"How do we stop it hallucinating?"::
You constrain it with retrieval and you let it refuse. Show Lab 04, step 4.

"Is our data used to train anything?"::
No. Inference runs on your hardware. Retrieval queries your corpus without
learning it. Tuning happens only on data you deliberately place in the taxonomy.

"Can we use a different model?"::
Yes. The serving layer is model-agnostic — KServe and vLLM will serve what you
bring. The support relationship differs, which is worth being precise about.

"What about FedRAMP?"::
Ask which boundary they are operating in before answering. The honest response
is that the platform runs inside accredited environments and the accreditation
belongs to the environment, not to the software.

"Is there an in-person version of this?"::
Tease it, do not date it. A larger on-site follow-on at New Carrollton in late
October or early November is being planned on Ted Craig's side, and nothing
about it goes on a slide yet. Say "watch for an in-person session this fall"
and leave it there.

== Facilitator's honesty checklist

Say these out loud during the session. They buy more credibility than any demo.

* The sample notice content is illustrative, written for the exercise. No agency
  reviewed it.
* The numbers in simulated output are representative of a working environment,
  not measurements from a named deployment.
* Exam selection is deliberately not on the list of good first candidates.
  Determinations affecting a taxpayer need governance long before automation.
* Every workflow in the deck keeps a named human on the signature. The model
  drafts; it never sends.
