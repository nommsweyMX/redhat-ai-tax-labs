= Lab 04 — Ground answers in your own guidance

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Product   | {rhoai} with a pgvector store
| Time      | About 5 minutes
| Script    | `labs/lab04-rag/run.sh`
| Outcome   | Answers that carry their citation — and a refusal when they cannot
|===

== Objective

A model that sounds confident and cites nothing is a liability in a tax agency.
Put your published guidance behind the model with retrieval, so every answer
returns the passage it came from — and so a question the corpus cannot answer
produces a referral rather than a guess.

== The refusal is the point

Most retrieval demos show the good answer. This lab spends half its time on the
bad question, because that is the behaviour that decides whether a governance
board will let this near a taxpayer.

`labs/lab04-rag/query.py` carries a `RELEVANCE_THRESHOLD` of 0.75. Below it the
script refuses, recommends referral to a specialist, and exits with code 3 so a
calling workflow can route the contact to a human. A retrieval system that
always answers is worse than no retrieval system, because it launders a guess
into something that looks sourced.

Step 5 prints the retrieval trace, showing every rejected candidate and its
score. Show that screen to your assessors. It turns "the model refused" into
"here is exactly why, and here is the number you can change."

== Prerequisites

For live mode: an OpenShift cluster, `psycopg` installed, an embeddings
endpoint (the one from <<lab-01>> or <<lab-03>>), and a corpus of your own
guidance in `labs/lab04-rag/corpus/` as `.txt`, `.md`, `.adoc` or `.html`.

[source,bash]
----
cd labs/lab04-rag
LAB_PACE=step ./run.sh

# Chunk your own corpus without touching a database
python3 ingest.py --source ./corpus --chunk 800 --overlap 120 --dry-run
----

== Walkthrough

[cols="1,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Step | Command | What to look for

| 1 | `oc apply -f manifests/pgvector.yaml` | The vector store runs on the same cluster as the model. Nothing leaves it.
| 2 | `python3 ingest.py` | Chunk counts per publication. Re-run this when guidance is republished.
| 3 | `python3 query.py '...CP14...'` | A correct answer *and* a `sources:` block with scores.
| 4 | `python3 query.py '...2031...'` | A refusal, and a recommendation to refer. Exit code 3.
| 5 | `python3 query.py --explain` | The retrieval trace: every candidate, its score, and the threshold.
|===

== Design notes worth arguing about

Chunking is deliberately boring::
Fixed window with overlap, preferring a paragraph break near the edge.
Published guidance is already structured into short numbered sections, and
clever splitters tend to break that structure rather than respect it.

Retrieval keeps sensitive text out of training::
The corpus is queried, never learned. That distinction matters enormously to
a data governance review, and it is worth stating explicitly in writing.

Guidance changes more often than weights should::
Re-ingesting a republished publication is minutes. Retraining is not. When the
answer needs to change because the rule changed, retrieval is the right layer.

== Troubleshooting

`psycopg is not installed`::
`pip install 'psycopg[binary]'`, or run the lab in simulate mode.

Embeddings endpoint unreachable::
The scripts default to `http://127.0.0.1:8000/v1/embeddings`. Override with
`EMBED_ENDPOINT`, and confirm the model from <<lab-01>> is still serving.

Everything scores below the threshold::
Either the corpus does not cover the question, or the embedding model differs
between ingest and query. Those must match — a mismatch produces uniformly low
scores that look like an empty corpus.

Answers cite the right passage but read poorly::
That is a prompt problem, not a retrieval problem. The system prompt is at the
top of `query.py`.
