= Lab 02 — Teach it your notice taxonomy

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Product   | SDG Hub + Training Hub on Red Hat AI
| Time      | About 6 minutes to walk; hours of GPU time to run for real
| Script    | `labs/lab02-instructlab/run.sh`
| Outcome   | A tuned model that knows your notice families, queues and house style
|===

== Objective

The base model from <<lab-01>> does not know your agency's notice codes, your
routing queues or how your correspondence is meant to read. Add that knowledge
as seed YAML a subject matter expert can write and a reviewer can approve,
generate synthetic training data from it with the SDG Hub library, fine-tune
with Training Hub, and prove the result is better than what you started with.

== Why this matters more than it looks

Domain adaptation is usually where AI programmes stall. The conventional path
needs a labelled dataset, which needs annotators, which needs a budget and six
months. The seed-example approach inverts it: a handful of examples written by
the people who already own the knowledge become thousands of training samples,
and the contribution is reviewed like code. (This workflow began life in
InstructLab; the retired `ilab` CLI is gone, and its ideas carry forward as
the composable `sdg_hub` and `training_hub` Python libraries.)

The file at
`labs/lab02-instructlab/taxonomy/knowledge/gov/tax/notices/qna.yaml` is worth
reading in full. It is deliberately written the way a real contribution would
be — plain language, one notice family per context block, and a house-style
section that captures the rules a correspondence reviewer applies by instinct.

== Prerequisites

For live mode: a GPU host, Python with the `sdg-hub` and `training-hub`
libraries, the <<lab-01>> endpoint running as the teacher model, and several
hours of GPU time for the training step.

[source,bash]
----
cd labs/lab02-instructlab
LAB_PACE=step ./run.sh
----

== Walkthrough

[cols="1,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Step | Command | What to look for

| 1
| `cat .../qna.yaml`
| Knowledge written as prose, not as feature vectors. A tax specialist can
  review this. That is the whole design goal.

| 2
| `python3 check_seeds.py`
| Schema validation. *Put this step in CI.* A malformed contribution should
  fail in a pull request, not after four hours of GPU time.

| 3
| `python3 generate.py --endpoint http://127.0.0.1:8000/v1`
| 12 seed examples becoming roughly 2,200 kept samples after quality
  filtering, with the <<lab-01>> endpoint as the teacher. Every sample traces
  back to something a human wrote.

| 4
| `python3 train.py --method lora`
| Loss dropping across three epochs. The checkpoint path is what you promote.

| 5
| `python3 evaluate.py`
| The comparison table against the base model. This is the artifact your
  governance board actually wants.
|===

== What good looks like

The evaluation table shows meaningful gains on agency-specific tasks — notice
family accuracy, correct routing queue, citing the right form — and a smaller
gain on general readability. That pattern is the healthy one. If general
benchmarks move a lot, you have probably overfitted to the seed examples.

.Representative evaluation output
[cols="2,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Measure | Base | Tuned | Delta

| Notice family accuracy       | 0.61 | 0.94 | +0.33
| Correct routing queue        | 0.58 | 0.91 | +0.33
| Cites the right form         | 0.44 | 0.89 | +0.45
| Plain-language readability   | 0.72 | 0.86 | +0.14
| Overall judge score          | 6.1  | 7.8  | +1.7
|===

IMPORTANT: Keep the held-out evaluation set *out* of the seed data. If the
questions you evaluate on are in the material you generated training data from,
the numbers are meaningless and someone will eventually notice.

== Troubleshooting

`check_seeds.py` reports a schema error::
Check `version: 3` at the top and the indentation under `questions_and_answers`.
Nearly every failure is one of those two.

Data generation produces very few samples::
The quality filter is discarding them. Usually the seed contexts are too short
or too similar to each other. Aim for a paragraph of real context per example.
Also confirm the teacher endpoint from <<lab-01>> is up — generation quality
is bounded by the teacher.

Training runs out of memory::
Lower the effective batch size, or use the LoRA method rather than full
fine-tuning. The lab uses LoRA rank 32.

The tuned model regresses on general questions::
Too many epochs, or too narrow a seed set. Add skills examples alongside
knowledge examples.
