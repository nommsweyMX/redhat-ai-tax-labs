= Lab 05 — Automate the toil around the model

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Product   | {aap} with {eda}
| Rung      | executes judgement
| Time      | About 5 minutes
| Script    | `labs/lab05-ansible/run.sh`
| Outcome   | Saturation detected and remediated in about a minute, with an audit trail
|===

== Objective

Most of the effort in production AI is not the model. It is provisioning,
patching, scaling and evidence collection. Wire a health check that tests the
endpoint the way a taxpayer would, then an {eda} rulebook that remediates
saturation before anyone opens a ticket.

On the ladder this lab executes judgement: the rulebook carries out decisions
the platform team already made — which conditions to remediate and which to
escalate — and it never makes a new one, which is exactly why the provenance
rule pages a person instead of redeploying.

== The rulebook is the runbook

`labs/lab05-ansible/rulebooks/inference_saturation.yml` maps four conditions to
four job templates. There is no separate runbook document to go stale, because
this file *is* the runbook and it executes.

One rule in it is deliberately different from the others. When the served model
digest stops matching the approved change record, the rulebook escalates rather
than self-remediating:

[source,yaml]
----
- name: Served model digest no longer matches the change record
  condition: event.alert.labels.alertname == "ModelProvenanceMismatch"
  action:
    run_job_template:
      # Deliberately does NOT self-remediate. A provenance mismatch is a
      # security event: page a human, do not quietly redeploy over it.
      name: "Escalate provenance mismatch"
----

Automatically redeploying over a provenance mismatch would destroy the evidence
of what was actually running. That is a decision worth making explicitly, and
worth defending in a design review.

== The digest check people forget

The health check playbook does four things, and the fourth is the one that
usually gets left out: it asserts that the model actually being served matches
the digest in the approved change record. A model that has drifted from the
approved artifact is an accreditation finding, not an incident, and it will be
found by an assessor if it is not found by automation first.

== Prerequisites

For live mode: {aap} 2.4 or later, `ansible-navigator` and `ansible-rulebook`
on the path, the `ansible.eda` collection, an Automation Controller with the
named job templates, and Alertmanager configured to post to the rulebook
source.

[source,bash]
----
cd labs/lab05-ansible
LAB_PACE=step ./run.sh
----

== Walkthrough

[cols="1,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Step | Command | What to look for

| 1
| `ansible-navigator run playbooks/model_endpoint_health.yml`
| Four tasks: the model list, a canary inquiry, the latency assertion, and the
  digest match against `{change-record}`.

| 2
| `cat rulebooks/inference_saturation.yml`
| Four conditions, four job templates, one of which deliberately escalates
  instead of fixing.

| 3
| `ansible-rulebook --rulebook ...`
| An alert at 14:38:02, remediation launched at 14:38:03, resolved at 14:39:04.
  Sixty-two seconds, no pager.

| 4
| `awx job list`
| The audit trail, with a launching identity on every row — including
  `eda-rulebook` for the automated action.
|===

== What good looks like

The alert-to-resolved interval is under two minutes and no human touched it.
More importantly, the `awx job list` output at the end is a complete record of
what changed, when, and what launched it — assembled continuously rather than
reconstructed at the end of a quarter.

That job list is the evidence <<lab-06>> depends on. Automation and compliance
are the same investment described from two directions.

== Troubleshooting

`ansible-rulebook: command not found`::
Install `ansible-rulebook` and a JDK — the rules engine needs Java. Or run in
simulate mode.

Rulebook starts but no events arrive::
Confirm Alertmanager is posting to the rulebook host and port, and that nothing
between them is dropping the webhook.

Job template not found::
The names in the rulebook must match the job templates in Controller exactly,
including the organization.

The canary assertion fails intermittently::
`canary_threshold_seconds` is 2.0 in the playbook. If the endpoint is genuinely
that slow under load, that is a capacity finding — raising the threshold hides
it rather than fixing it.
