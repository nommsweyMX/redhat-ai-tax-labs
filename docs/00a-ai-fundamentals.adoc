= AI fundamentals — the theory, demystified

Everything in the labs works better when the room shares a mental model of
what a language model actually does. This chapter is that mental model, in
plain language. Nothing in it requires mathematics beyond multiplication.

== What actually happens when you ask

Take the question a facilitator asks in Lab 01: _"Find the missing document
for this contact."_ Four things happen, and only four.

. *Your words become tokens.* The model never sees words. Text is split into
  small pieces called tokens — roughly four characters each — and every token
  becomes a number. "Form 8822" is three tokens; a page of a notice is about
  700.
. *The model scores what could come next.* One pass through billions of
  learned numbers — the _weights_ — turns your tokens into a probability for
  every possible next token.
. *It writes one token at a time.* The winning token is appended to the input
  and the whole pass runs again, until the answer is complete. That is all
  "generation" is.
. *Temperature is the dial.* At 0.1 the model almost always takes the top
  candidate: predictable and repeatable, which is what tax administration
  wants.

The demystifying sentence, worth saying out loud in the session: when Lab 01
returns `missing_doc: Form 8822`, that answer was not fetched from any system.
It is the most probable continuation of the contact text under those weights —
nothing else happened, and nothing else needed to.

== Where the answer comes from

The question underneath most audience confusion is: _"do I need to do
anything to get that response?"_ The answer is no — sending the prompt *is*
the request. There are exactly three places an answer can draw from:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Source | What it contributes

| The weights
| Patterns learned in training, then frozen. Not a database — nothing is
  looked up, nothing is fetched from anywhere. Lab 02 is the only lab that
  changes them.

| The prompt
| Everything you send: the taxpayer's message plus your instructions. The
  system prompt in Lab 01's `inquiry.json` is what names the fields to fill —
  read it and the output format stops being mysterious.

| Retrieval (optional)
| Your own documents, found by meaning and placed into the prompt. This is
  how answers arrive with citations — or refuse honestly. Lab 04 builds it.
|===

The prompt and any retrieved passages share the _context window_ — the
model's short-term memory, cleared after every request.

Three reassurances follow directly from this mechanism, and they are worth
stating explicitly to any audience handling taxpayer data:

* *Nothing leaves.* No internet call happens at answer time. On this platform
  the model runs on your hardware — Lab 01 proves it with one line of `ss`
  output.
* *Nothing is learned from you.* A prompt does not train the model. Weights
  change only in deliberate tuning, with data you choose and review.
* *Confident nonsense is possible.* Plausible tokens can assemble a wrong
  answer. That is why retrieval cites, thresholds refuse, and a human signs.

== How this maps onto the labs

[cols="1,4", options="header"]
|===
| Lab | The theory it demonstrates

| 01 | Pure inference: tokens in, predicted tokens out, steered by a system
      prompt. The `tokens: 214 in / 96 out` line is the whole mechanism made
      visible.
| 02 | The only weight change in the series: a LoRA adapter tuned with
      Training Hub on synthetic data SDG Hub expanded from reviewed seed
      examples. Explains why the base model scored 0.61 on notice accuracy
      and the tuned one 0.94 — CP2000 routing was never in the base training
      data.
| 03 | Inference at scale: GPUs matter because each pass is billions of
      multiplications; vLLM batches many requests through each pass; the
      90-second cold start is loading 8 GB of weights into GPU memory.
| 04 | Embeddings place similar meanings near each other in a vector space;
      retrieval finds the nearest guidance passages and puts them in the
      context window. Below the similarity threshold, the honest output is a
      refusal.
| 05 | No machine learning at all — deterministic automation around the model.
      The intelligence stays in the model; the reliability lives in the
      automation.
| 06 | Weights are frozen, so a deployed model cannot decay — drift metrics
      detect the *world* changing, not the model. And a model is ultimately a
      file: hash it, sign it, verify it.
| 07 | No new machine learning at all: Lab 04's retrieval pattern pointed at
      a different corpus. Logs, runbooks and postmortems become vectors; the
      question finds the nearest evidence; the model assembles a cited
      diagnosis. The hours lived in diagnosis — grounded retrieval removes
      them.
|===

== Glossary

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Term | Plain-language meaning

| Token | The small piece of text a model actually reads and writes — roughly
  four characters. "Form 8822" is three tokens.
| Tokenization | Splitting text into tokens and turning each into a number —
  the only translation step between words and mathematics.
| Weights (parameters) | The billions of numbers learned during training.
  Patterns of language, not a database of facts; frozen once deployed.
| Inference | Running the model: tokens in, one predicted token out, repeated.
| Prompt | Everything you send — instructions plus the text to work on.
  Sending it is the whole request.
| Context window | How much the model can consider at once. Short-term
  memory, cleared after every request.
| Temperature | How adventurous the next-token choice is. Near zero:
  repeatable — the right setting for tax work.
| Embedding | Text converted into a vector, where similar meanings sit close
  together. What makes searching by meaning possible.
| Vector store | A database of embeddings — Labs 04 and 07 run one in pgvector.
| RAG | Retrieval-augmented generation: fetch relevant passages, place them
  into the prompt, require the answer to cite them.
| Fine-tuning (LoRA) | Deliberately adjusting weights with your examples.
  LoRA trains a small add-on instead of all weights — cheaper, reversible.
| Hallucination | A confident answer assembled from plausible tokens with
  nothing behind it. Countered by retrieval, thresholds, refusal, and a human
  signature.
| Drift | The input mix changing versus the accredited baseline. The world
  changed — not the model. Weights cannot rot.
|===
