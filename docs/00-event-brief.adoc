= Event brief

== The operating reality

Tax administration has a shape that makes it unusually well suited to language
models, and unusually unforgiving of the way they are normally deployed.

Demand is seasonal.:: Filing season concentrates a year of volume into weeks.
Capacity that sits idle in August cannot be justified; capacity that runs out
in April becomes a headline.

The rules are locked in legacy code.:: Notice generation, eligibility and
offset logic live in systems written decades ago. Rewriting them is a
decade-long programme. Wrapping them in intelligent workflows is a quarter.

The answers are in the text.:: Correspondence, transcripts, published guidance
and case notes are unstructured. Traditional analytics never reached them.
Language models are built for exactly this material.

The data cannot leave.:: Federal tax information carries handling rules that
rule out sending prompts to a public endpoint. The model has to come to the
data — on premises, in an accredited cloud region, or in an air-gapped enclave.

That last constraint is the one that decides the architecture. Choosing a model
is a six-month decision. Choosing where models run, how they are governed and
how they scale is a ten-year one.

== Where AI lands in the filing lifecycle

[cols="1,3,1", options="header"]
|===
| Stage | Work | Good first candidate?

| Intake and classification
| Route paper, portal and phone contacts by intent rather than keyword
| Yes

| Validation
| Flag inconsistencies and missing documentation before a case is opened
| Yes

| Correspondence
| Draft plain-language notices for a reviewer to edit and sign
| Yes

| Exam selection
| Determinations affecting a specific taxpayer
| *No* — governance first, automation much later

| Collections support
| Summarize case history so an officer opens a case already briefed
| Yes

| Taxpayer service
| Answer from published guidance, with the citation attached
| Yes
|===

Two rules of thumb come out of that table.

. *Start where a human already reviews the output.* If a person signs the letter
  today, a model that drafts it changes throughput without changing
  accountability. That is a defensible first deployment.
. *Automate the toil around the model, not just the model.* Most of the effort
  in production AI is provisioning, patching, scaling and evidence collection.
  That is an automation problem, and it is already solved.

== Reference architecture

The platform is four horizontal layers with two concerns running vertically
through all of them.

.Layers, bottom to top
[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Layer | What it provides

| {rhel} and the {rhaiis}
| Bare metal GPU nodes, virtualized datacenter, accredited public cloud or a
  classified enclave. Granite models, accelerator drivers and the vLLM-based
  inference server ship as one supported stack.

| {ocp}
| Scheduling, GPU partitioning, network policy, multi-site placement and GitOps
  delivery. The same cluster runs the modernized case-management applications
  beside the models they call.

| {rhoai}
| Model serving on KServe and vLLM, tuning and evaluation pipelines, a model
  registry with versions and lineage, and retrieval against a vector store.

| Mission applications
| Correspondence intake, notice drafting, case summarization, guidance
  assistant. Systems of record stay where they are, reached through APIs.
|===

.Concerns, running through every layer
Automation:: {aap} and {eda} provision, patch, remediate and collect evidence.
Covered in <<lab-05>>.

Trust:: Signed models, FIPS-validated cryptography, SELinux, the Compliance
Operator, TrustyAI drift and fairness monitoring, and disconnected mirroring.
Covered in <<lab-06>>.

The point of drawing the two concerns vertically is that neither is a layer you
add at the end. A project that treats compliance as a phase after deployment
discovers, at the worst possible moment, that it cannot produce evidence for
anything that already happened.

== The questions an authorizing official will ask

[cols="2,3,1", options="header"]
|===
| Question | Platform control | Where

| Where does our data go when someone prompts the model?
| Nowhere. Inference runs on your cluster, in your enclave, on your accelerators.
| <<lab-01>>

| Can you prove this model is the one we approved?
| Model artifacts are signed and verified before admission to the cluster.
| <<lab-06>>

| Is the cryptography validated?
| FIPS mode is a supported operating state of the OS and the platform above it.
| <<lab-06>>

| How do we know it stays compliant next quarter?
| Scheduled scans against a hardening profile, with machine-readable results.
| <<lab-06>>

| How do we detect the model drifting or skewing?
| Continuous drift and fairness metrics on live inference traffic.
| <<lab-06>>

| What if we have no internet at all?
| Mirror images and models into the enclave; the platform is built for
  disconnected operation.
| <<lab-01>>

| Who is accountable when it is wrong?
| A named reviewer. The deployment patterns here keep a person on the signature.
| <<lab-04>>
|===

== Adoption path

.Prove — days 0 to 90
* Stand up one supported endpoint with the {rhaiis}
* Agree the accelerator capacity plan and where it physically sits
* Settle which data classes may be used, in writing
* Pick one text-heavy workflow that already has a human reviewer
* Measure the baseline before changing anything

.Ground — days 90 to 180
* Move serving onto {rhoai} with autoscaling
* Add retrieval over your own published guidance
* Tune on agency vocabulary with SDG Hub and Training Hub
* Wire drift, fairness and compliance monitoring
* Automate provisioning and patching end to end

.Scale — days 180 and beyond
* Promote through a model registry with real approvals
* Add workflows two through five on the same platform
* Prove the filing-season surge with a load rehearsal
* Extend to the disconnected or classified enclave
* Report outcomes in mission terms, not token counts

TIP: Most programmes stall on the second and third bullets of the Prove phase —
no accelerator capacity plan, and no written agreement on which data may be
used. Neither is a technical problem, and both take longer than the pilot.
