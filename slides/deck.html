The operating reality

The work is seasonal, textual and unforgiving

Demand

Peak load, fixed staff

Idle capacity in August is unjustifiable; exhausted capacity in April is a headline.

Systems

Rules locked in legacy code

Rewriting the systems of record is a decade. Wrapping them in intelligent workflows is a quarter.

Data

The answers are in the text

Correspondence, transcripts and case notes are unstructured — exactly what language models are built for.

The constraint that decides everything

The data cannot leave

FTI handling rules rule out public endpoints. The model comes to the data — on premises, accredited cloud, or air-gapped enclave.

Why it matters

From data to judgement — and who climbs each step

Why it matters

The backlog lives at the bottom of the ladder

Transcribing, matching, routing and summarizing consume the workforce. Every hour a machine absorbs down there is an hour an examiner spends deciding — the only work that resolves a case.

Why it matters

Judgement is deliberately never automated

The model classifies, grounds and drafts; a named person decides and signs. That is a design rule, not a technical limit — and it is what makes the deployment defensible to counsel and oversight.

Why it matters

Predictive and generative AI answer different questions

One produces a number from structured history. The other produces language. They fail differently — so they are governed differently.

They compose

Predictive picks the case, generative prepares it

A risk score decides which case deserves attention; a language model assembles the brief, the draft and the citations; a person decides. The strongest workflows chain all three.

Same platform

One cluster serves both, side by side

A gradient-boosted scorer and a Granite language model deploy through the same registry, share the same GPUs and land in the same audit trail — that is the point of the platform (→ Lab 03).

Where AI actually lands in the filing lifecycle

Highlighted stages already have a human reviewer in the loop — which makes them the honest places to start.

Rule of thumb

Start where a human already reviews the output

If a person signs the letter today, a model that drafts it changes throughput without changing accountability. That is a defensible first deployment.

Rule of thumb

Automate the toil around the model, not just the model

Most of the effort in production AI is provisioning, patching, scaling and evidence collection. That is an automation problem, and it is solved.

The platform on its own data

Point the same pattern at your logs — MTTR is the proof

The Lab 04 retrieval stack, aimed at operations: logs, runbooks, incident history and change records become a private notebook the on-call engineer can question — on the cluster, inside the boundary.

Why a notebook, not a dashboard

Dashboards answer anticipated questions

A notebook over your own data answers the one you did not anticipate — “what changed, has this happened before, which runbook applies” — in language, with citations you check before acting.

Why it must be local

Logs are FTI-adjacent

They carry hostnames, account fragments and case identifiers. The notebook runs on the same OpenShift install it observes — nothing is exported to anyone’s hosted service.

Why it transfers

Same stack as the taxpayer system

The team that grounds answers for operations (Lab 07) has already built the taxpayer-facing version (Lab 04) — one retrieval pattern, two corpora, one governance story.

Adoption path

Ninety days, then ninety more

Nothing here requires a rewrite of a system of record. Each phase produces something you can show.

ProveDays 0–90
  • Stand up one supported inference endpoint on RHEL AI
  • Agree the accelerator capacity plan and where it physically sits
  • Settle which data classes may be used, in writing
  • Pick one text-heavy workflow with a human already reviewing
  • Measure the baseline before you change anything
GroundDays 90–180
  • Move serving onto OpenShift AI with autoscaling
  • Add retrieval over your own published guidance
  • Tune on agency vocabulary with InstructLab
  • Wire drift, fairness and compliance monitoring
  • Automate provisioning and patching end to end
ScaleDays 180+
  • Promote through a model registry with real approvals
  • Add workflows two through five on the same platform
  • Prove the filing-season surge with a load rehearsal
  • Extend to the disconnected or classified enclave
  • Report outcomes in mission terms, not token counts
