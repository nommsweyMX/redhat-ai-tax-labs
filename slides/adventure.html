The one thing to remember

The ladder: data → information → knowledge → judgement

Every technology, slide and lab in the kit is tagged with the rung it serves — in rainbow order, so nobody has to learn a palette. AI moves work up the ladder; it never takes the top step. See the slide · read the definitions · the machine-readable map.

Data

What happened, uninterpreted: returns, transcripts, calls, logs, telemetry. Where it lives decides the architecture.

Information

Data in context for one case: a classification, a score, a diagnosis, a metric. What predictive AI and inference produce.

Knowledge

Information that informs an outcome: guidance, precedent, runbooks, taxonomies. The institution's memory, retrievable and cited.

Judgement

A determination someone signs. Deliberately human; automation executes what was decided, it never decides.

01

Serve a model inside your boundary

data → informationAbout 5 minutes (5 steps)

For Platform and infrastructure engineers; anyone who has to answer "where does our data go when someone prompts the model?"

Pull the signed Red Hat AI Inference Server (vLLM) image, download the validated RedHatAI/granite-3.3-8b-instruct model, serve it on localhost, and classify a real taxpayer refund inquiry without any inference call leaving the host.

The screens people remember

  • ss -ltnp shows 127.0.0.1:8000 — the endpoint has no egress path; that screen is the point
  • podman pull prints 'Signature verified — policy: signedBy release key' on the way in
  • The curl reply returns category, routing queue, missing Form 8822 and a draft for a human to sign
  • assets/inquiry.json's system prompt forbids stating a determination as final

To run it for real LAB_MODE=simulate: nothing. LAB_MODE=live: a RHEL host with a supported accelerator and CDI devices generated (nvidia-ctk cdi generate), podman, huggingface-cli, curl and jq on the path, registry.redhat.io credentials, ~20 GB free disk. Step 3 blocks in live mode; run it in a second terminal.

02

Teach it your notice taxonomy

knowledgeAbout 6 minutes (5 steps); hours of GPU to run live

For Subject matter experts and data leads who own notice families, routing rules and correspondence house style

Write agency knowledge as reviewable seed YAML (qna.yaml, schema v3), validate it, expand 12 seed examples into ~2,200 kept samples with SDG Hub, LoRA-tune Granite with Training Hub, and prove the tuned model beats the base.

The screens people remember

  • 12 seed examples become 2,556 generated, 2,203 kept — every sample traces to a human-written seed
  • Evaluation table: notice family accuracy 0.61 → 0.94, cites the right form 0.44 → 0.89
  • check_seeds.py is the CI gate: fail in a pull request, not after four hours of GPU
  • Keep the held-out evaluation set out of the seed data or the numbers mean nothing

To run it for real LAB_MODE=simulate: only python3 (and only checked in live mode). LAB_MODE=live: a GPU host, Python with the sdg-hub and training-hub libraries, the Lab 01 endpoint (http://127.0.0.1:8000/v1) running as the teacher model, and several hours of GPU time. Note: check_seeds.py, generate.py, train.py and evaluate.py are not shipped in the lab directory — only taxonomy/knowledge/gov/tax/notices/qna.yaml is.

Survive filing season

information at scaleAbout 6 minutes (6 steps)

For Platform engineers and capacity planners responsible for taxpayer-facing services at April peak

Deploy the Lab 02 model as a KServe InferenceService on the vllm-runtime, watch it become Ready in ~96 seconds, then run a 400-concurrent-user rehearsal and watch replicas climb 2 → 20 and back with no application change.

The screens people remember

  • hey -z 90s -c 400: 1,183 req/s, p95 0.612s, zero errors, replicas 2 → 20 → 2
  • manifests/inferenceservice-tax-classifier.yaml is the capacity plan: minReplicas 2, scaleMetric concurrency, scaleTarget 24
  • storageUri pinned to sha256:4b91c0e2f7a3 — the digest the authorizing official approved, not a tag
  • Scale-to-two, not zero: cold-starting an 8B model costs ~90 seconds

To run it for real LAB_MODE=simulate: nothing. LAB_MODE=live: an OpenShift cluster with Red Hat OpenShift AI installed, GPU nodes with the NVIDIA GPU Operator, oc authenticated, and hey for the load test. WARNING: step 6 consumes every GPU the namespace can schedule for 90 seconds — non-production cluster only.

04

Ground answers in your own guidance

knowledgeAbout 5 minutes (5 steps)

For Governance boards, correspondence reviewers and anyone deciding whether an AI answer may reach a taxpayer

Stand up pgvector on the same cluster as the model, ingest Publications 17/501/594 plus internal notice guidance (3,135 chunks), get a cited CP14 answer, then watch the system refuse a 2031 question and show exactly why.

The screens people remember

  • The refusal: 'sources: none above threshold (0.75)' — refer to a specialist, exit code 3
  • --explain prints every rejected candidate with its score against the 0.750 threshold
  • Every good answer carries a sources block: Publication 594 p.4, score 0.891
  • The corpus is queried, never learned — sensitive text stays out of training
  • The run.sh has a fifth step, the --explain trace; the deck version stops at four

To run it for real LAB_MODE=simulate: nothing. LAB_MODE=live: an OpenShift cluster, psycopg installed, an embeddings endpoint (Lab 01 or Lab 03; default http://127.0.0.1:8000/v1/embeddings), and your own guidance in labs/lab04-rag/corpus/ as .txt/.md/.adoc/.html. ingest.py --dry-run chunks a corpus with no database.

Prove it to your ISSO

evidence → judgementAbout 6 minutes (5 steps)

For ISSOs, authorizing officials, assessors and security architects accrediting an AI workload

Run the four checks an authorizing official actually runs: cosign-verify the model against change record CR-8841, confirm FIPS and SELinux enforcing, read a real Compliance Operator result, and interpret TrustyAI drift and fairness metrics correctly.

bin/lib.sh — the shared lab runtime (simulate vs live)

5

How every lab is a sequence of step calls — a real command plus its expected output — and how four environment variables switch mode, pacing, speed and colour.

  • Defaults from the file: LAB_MODE=simulate, LAB_PACE=auto, LAB_SPEED=0.04; NO_COLOR unset (any value, or a non-tty stdout, disables colour).
  • step "cmd" <<'OUT' ... OUT: simulate prints expected lines; live evals the command and on non-zero exit shows the expected output as the finding.
  • LAB_PACE=step prompts '[Enter] to run, s to skip, q to quit'; LAB_STEPS_TOTAL and LAB_PROMPT are per-lab knobs.
  • require_cmd exits 2 in live mode when a tool is missing; lab_footer returns 1 if any live step failed.

For Facilitators tuning a demo, and anyone writing a new lab.

.gitattributes and .gitignore — LF enforcement and what never gets committed

1

Why the lab scripts run identically on every machine: every textual file is forced to LF, and lab working directories and the guidance corpus are excluded from git.

  • * text=auto eol=lf, with explicit LF for .sh .py .yml .yaml .adoc .json .ini and Makefile; .pptx .png .pdf binary.
  • Ignored: build/, __pycache__, labs/*/sdg/, labs/*/checkpoints/, labs/*/.venv/, labs/lab04-rag/corpus/* (except README.txt).
  • Rationale in the file: CRLF in a shell script is a runtime error that only shows up on someone else's machine.

labs/lab04-rag/ingest.py — chunk, embed and index guidance into pgvector

knowledge5

A deliberately boring ingestion pipeline: fixed-window chunks with overlap that prefer paragraph breaks, embedded via an OpenAI-compatible endpoint and upserted into a pgvector table with an HNSW cosine index.

  • Defaults: --source ./corpus, --chunk 800, --overlap 120, --embed-model granite-embedding-125m, --batch 32; reads .txt .md .adoc .html .htm.
  • --dry-run (used in the lab) walks the corpus and reports chunk counts with no psycopg, endpoint or database.
  • Creates table tax_guidance(uid, source, ordinal, body, embedding vector(768)) and index tax_guidance_hnsw with vector_cosine_ops.
  • Env: EMBED_ENDPOINT default http://127.0.0.1:8000/v1/embeddings; PGHOST PGPORT PGDATABASE(tax_guidance) PGUSER PGPASSWORD.

For Data and application teams building retrieval over published guidance.

labs/lab04-rag/query.py — cited answers, and the refusal that is the point

5

Retrieval-augmented answering where the important behaviour is refusing: below a relevance threshold it says it has no guidance, recommends referral, and exits 3 so a workflow can route to a human.

  • RELEVANCE_THRESHOLD = 0.75, TOP_K = 6; score is 1 - cosine distance from pgvector's <=> operator.
  • --explain prints the retrieval trace — every candidate, its score, accepted/REJECTED, the threshold and the decision — for a governance board.
  • System prompt has four rules: only the passages, cite every claim, say so and refer if unanswered, never state a taxpayer determination as final.
  • Defaults: --model granite-8b-tax-v1, temperature 0.1, max_tokens 600, INFERENCE_ENDPOINT http://127.0.0.1:8000/v1/chat/completions.

For Data teams and governance staff asking how hallucination is stopped.

The talk track — five planted questions

The exchanges Jon and Brad rehearsed, and the answers. They work just as well in your own briefing.

planted question

“So is AI taking our jobs?”

Jon asks; Brad answers that AI is not coming for your job because right now it does not have judgement — the ladder's top step stays human.

  • The make-the-tests-pass story: told to make tests pass, the AI edits the code until they do — information, not judgement.
  • Optional garnish, attributed to Karpathy: you can outsource intelligence but not understanding.

planted question

“What’s a seam?”

Jon plays the foil; Brad answers with the pants analogy — failures happen at integration seams between systems, never in the middle where the fabric is strongest.

  • RHEL and OpenShift interoperating layer to layer is what makes the seams boring.
  • 'We are not the only ones who do it — but we are uniquely positioned.'
Open the console
planted question

“What is fine-tuning to you, Jon?”

Brad cold-asks Jon on the architecture slide; the answer reprises 'fine-tuning is studying for the test, retrieval is bringing the notebook' and turns information into actionable knowledge.

  • Jon calls 'how do you turn information into actionable knowledge' the core of the whole talk.
  • Fine-tuning is durable behavior — never facts that change each filing season.
Open the console
planted question

“What do you actually mean by lock-in?”

Jon plays the foil ('I thought we already bought the software'); Brad answers with the phone-plan analogy: modern lock-in limits future decisions without you knowing.

  • A plan where you cannot call Pizza Hut, only Domino's — real lock-in is bespoke hyperscaler-unique services.
  • Honest caveat said out loud: sometimes you legitimately need a native service; the ask is only infrastructure as code.
Open the console
planted question

“What is a predicament versus a problem?”

Jon asks; Brad answers that a problem you solve today while a predicament you can only plan around — so a hypothetical GPU shortage must not stall the pilot.

  • Plan for problems, not predicaments: the problem now is grounding the proof so you can scale.
  • Success test: no decision made today may limit your options tomorrow.
