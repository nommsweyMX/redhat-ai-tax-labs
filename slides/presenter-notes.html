SLIDE 02 · AI IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Four different jobs get called “AI”

3–11 min block begins
InformationKnowledgeJudgement
On screenFour cards — Predictive (estimate), Generative/LLM (create), Agentic (reason + act), Automation (execute) — with analogies, and a dashed "what it is not" box.

Terminology comes first, by design — foundational context before any argument. Federal audiences hear "AI" used as if every technique is the same, and as if AI means the consumer tools — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot. It is neither. Predictive AI estimates. Generative AI creates. An LLM is a generative model specialized in language. Agentic systems add tools and rules. Automation executes known steps.

Walk the analogies — the weather forecast, the well-read drafting assistant, the junior analyst with a restricted tool belt, the checklist that performs the checklist (that is where Ansible belongs). The strongest mission pattern combines them.

Agentic AIknowledge → action

Land the not-boxPredictive AI is not a crystal ball. An LLM is not a database, a policy authority, or a source of truth. An agent is not permission to bypass controls.

SLIDE 05 · DATA TO JUDGEMENT

From data to judgement — and who climbs each step

DataInformationKnowledgeJudgementThe spine itself. Steps coloured by rung; risers coloured by the rung they deliver: predictive → information, generative + RAG → knowledge, people → judgement.
On screenFour-step staircase: Data → Information → Knowledge ("information that informs an outcome") → Judgement. Pills on the risers: predictive AI, generative AI + RAG, people. Top step marked "stays human."

Walk the stairs left to right. Brad’s line, said out loud: information is only knowledge when it informs a specific outcome. Knowledge has to drive action, not check a box — and most organizations stall exactly there.

Predictive models climb the first riser — score, match, flag. Generative models with retrieval climb the second — read, ground, draft. The top step is deliberately human — a determination someone signs. That is a design rule, not a technical limit, and it is what makes the deployment defensible.

The backlog lives at the bottom of the ladder. Every hour a machine absorbs down there is an hour an examiner spends deciding — the only work that resolves a case.

PLANTED — Jon asks it“So is AI taking our jobs?” — Brad answers: it is not coming for your job, because right now it does not have judgement. Jon’s read: everyone quietly feels this, which is why it is the masterpiece slide.
The make-the-tests-pass storyAsk an AI to “make all these tests pass” and it edits the code until they pass. It did exactly what you asked. That is information — not knowledge, and not judgement. Optional garnish, attributed: Karpathy — “you can outsource intelligence but you cannot outsource understanding.”
