SLIDE 02 · AI IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Four different jobs get called “AI”

3–11 min block begins
InformationKnowledgeJudgement
On screenFour cards — Predictive (estimate), Generative/LLM (create), Agentic (reason + act), Automation (execute) — with analogies, and a dashed "what it is not" box.

Terminology comes first, by design — foundational context before any argument. Federal audiences hear "AI" used as if every technique is the same, and as if AI means the consumer tools — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot. It is neither. Predictive AI estimates. Generative AI creates. An LLM is a generative model specialized in language. Agentic systems add tools and rules. Automation executes known steps.

Walk the analogies — the weather forecast, the well-read drafting assistant, the junior analyst with a restricted tool belt, the checklist that performs the checklist (that is where Ansible belongs). The strongest mission pattern combines them.

On the ladder
Predictive AIdata → information
Generative AI / LLMinformation → knowledge
Agentic AIknowledge → action
Automationexecutes judgement
Land the not-boxPredictive AI is not a crystal ball. An LLM is not a database, a policy authority, or a source of truth. An agent is not permission to bypass controls.
Land the on-ramp lineIf you are not automated, you are not going to have a good generative or predictive AI inside your boundary. Automation is the prerequisite.

SLIDE 03 · WHY NOW

The work is seasonal, textual and unforgiving

DataThe data cannot leave; the answers are in the text.
On screenDemand curve — April at 6× the August low, far above the flat staffed-capacity line — plus four cards ending on the red one: "the data cannot leave."

Do not lead with the technology. Lead with the shape of the problem: seasonal demand spikes against a fixed workforce, decades of correspondence logic locked in code nobody wants to touch, and data spread across systems that were never designed to talk.

The chart is the argument: a year of demand arrives in six weeks. The shaded gap is what automation absorbs. And the last card decides the architecture: federal tax information cannot go to a public endpoint. The model has to come to the data.

Pivot lineChoosing a model is a six-month decision. Choosing where models run, how they are governed and how they scale is a ten-year one.
Macy’s at Black FridayThe analogy: Macy’s at Thanksgiving, Black Friday and Christmas, a very seasonal workload against a fixed store. The off-season is not waste; it is when you rewrite and integrate the knowledge you gathered during the surge, so there is more headroom when the next surge comes. Idle capacity in August is only unjustifiable if you do nothing with it.
The snake eating its own tailThe closed loop has one rule: it feeds on data from YOUR systems of record, added to your knowledge base to automate it. Never information generated from information; that is the snake eating its own tail.
Use with tactAI is interesting here precisely because it works on unstructured language, which is most of what a tax agency actually handles. Large federal accounts are losing institutional knowledge as people age out or are RIF’d; capturing what the workforce knows is part of why this matters now.

SLIDE 04 · INSIDE AN LLM

An LLM is the engine — not the whole vehicle

DataInformationKnowledge
On screenOne central visual: the six-layer stack (mission app → context/RAG → model → inference → platform → compute), minimal text, and the test/notebook line beneath. The detail lives here, not on the slide.

Narrate the layers from the bottom up. The GPU is compute, and the unstructured logs and core dumps at that compute-and-data layer are what feed the platform. Inference is the running engine — vLLM, the Red Hat AI Inference Server. The model contains learned statistical patterns — not a live copy of IRS policy. Context is the open book we hand it for this request; RAG retrieves approved material into it. The mission app sits on top, at the judgement layer, where a person decides.

The three knobs, spoken rather than shown: prompting is better instructions for this request. RAG is the right reference material for this request. Fine-tuning changes learned behavior for future requests — use it for durable behavior, never for facts that change every filing season.

SLIDE 05 · DATA TO JUDGEMENT

From data to judgement — and who climbs each step

DataInformationKnowledgeJudgementThe spine itself. Steps coloured by rung; risers coloured by the rung they deliver: predictive → information, generative + RAG → knowledge, people → judgement.
On screenFour-step staircase: Data → Information → Knowledge ("information that informs an outcome") → Judgement. Pills on the risers: predictive AI, generative AI + RAG, people. Top step marked "stays human."

Walk the stairs left to right. Brad’s line, said out loud: we are really good at creating data and information and pretending it is knowledge. If it is not informing anything we need, it is not knowledge. Knowledge has to drive action, not check a box — and most organizations stall exactly there. Each rung is worth more to the mission.

Predictive models climb the first riser — score, match, flag. Generative models with retrieval climb the second — read, ground, draft. The top step is deliberately human — a determination someone signs. That is a design rule, not a technical limit, and it is what makes the deployment defensible.

The backlog lives at the bottom of the ladder. Every hour a machine absorbs down there is an hour an examiner spends deciding — the only work that resolves a case.

The masterpiece slide (Jon’s word): say the DIKW lineAnyone with ten or twenty years in tech will recognize the classic data-information-knowledge-insight progression. We replaced insight with judgement for the AI age, and judgement is the rung that matters most: it is the one a person signs.
Make it concrete: reboot every FridayKnowledge can be automated: reboot the mail server every Friday, patch the Windows devices every Tuesday; a directive, a mandate or an AO memo turned into a job. Judgement is knowing that a Tuesday when an AWS GovCloud region is down is not the time to run it. AI is not replacing that person; their call feeds back into the loop as the next piece of knowledge.
PLANTED — Jon asks it“So is AI taking our jobs?” — Brad answers: it is not coming for your job, because right now it does not have judgement. Jon’s read: everyone quietly feels this, which is why it is the masterpiece slide.
The make-the-tests-pass storyAsk an AI to “make all these tests pass” and it edits the code until they pass. It did exactly what you asked. That is information — not knowledge, and not judgement. Optional garnish, attributed: Karpathy — “you can outsource intelligence but you cannot outsource understanding.”

SLIDE 10 · WHY RED HAT AI

The federal problem is not “get a model.” It is operate AI without giving up control.

17–23 min block begins
DataJudgement
On screen"Bring the model to the mission" proposition (now with the glass-box line), Inference / Data / Agents / Platform strip, and six proof points including Models-as-a-Service and the modern-lock-in framing.

Do not sell a particular model as the moat. The value is operational control: choose the model, accelerator and location; keep data where authorization requires; run inference efficiently; govern agents and lifecycle; automate the surrounding environment.

Two lines to land: glass box, not black box — open source means you can inspect what the system is doing; and modern lock-in limits what you can build next — a worse trap than the old hardware kind, because it constrains future functionality, not just today’s invoice.

For the platform people in the room: Models-as-a-Service — a central team publishes governed model endpoints the projects consume, instead of every project building its own fragile AI stack.

The 10,000 seats storyAgencies buy ten thousand chatbot seats and start plugging things into them. What they are never shown is the alternative: running their own instances next to their own data — feeding self-healing, closed-loop systems of record, tied into operations, extended toward fraud, waste and abuse. The knowledge that matters lives in workflows, policies and procedures you cannot paste into a public chatbot.
Someone else’s computerCloud AI is ultimately just someone else’s computer. If it is your computer and your data, you can go check and verify — and if you are not convinced by the answers coming back, that is a waste of CPUs, GPUs and cooling. Colocation cuts the attack surface and the pivot points, and it makes the experience better: no chain of egresses, ingresses, F5s, LTMs and NAT hops adding transit time. If the first experience is not enjoyable, nobody comes back.
Differentiator vs discriminatorAutomation alone is a differentiator. The estate — the underlying OS, the interconnectability, the whole ecosystem, with an execution layer tying them together — is the discriminator nobody else has.
Say the two linesOne focal point, the red box in the middle: bring the model to the mission, not the mission data to somebody else’s model. Then: don’t move what’s hard; bring what’s easy to the data. And: what is unique is your data; you have it, you keep visibility into it, and you should never have to give it up. Keep the data on-prem and scale horizontally, or serve the model as a service locally; either way the data does not travel.
