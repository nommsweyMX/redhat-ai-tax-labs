== Turning AI strategy into tax administration outcomes

A secure, flexible foundation that connects automation, hybrid cloud and
intelligent workflows.

Then six labs where you run it yourself.

[.notes]
--
Open by naming the room: Red Hat, Four Inc. and Carahsoft. Frame the hour —
25 minutes of story, 30 minutes of live terminal, 5 for questions.

The promise: every claim we make today, we run in a shell before you leave.

== The work is seasonal, textual and unforgiving

[cols="1,1"]
|===
| *Demand* | Filing season concentrates a year of volume into weeks
| *Systems* | Notice and offset logic locked in decades-old code
| *Data* | The answers live in correspondence, not in tables
| *Constraint* | Federal tax information cannot go to a public endpoint
|===

[.notes]
--
Do not lead with the technology. Lead with the shape of the problem.

AI is interesting here precisely because it works on unstructured language —
which is most of what a tax agency actually handles.

The last row is the one that decides the architecture. The model has to come to
the data.
--

== So the platform is the decision

Choosing a model is a six-month decision.

Choosing *where models run, how they are governed and how they scale* is a
ten-year one.

[.notes]
--
This is the pivot of the whole deck. If they accept this sentence, everything
after it follows.
--

== Where AI lands in the filing lifecycle

[cols="2,1", options="header"]
|===
| Stage | Start here?

| Intake and classification | Yes
| Validation | Yes
| Correspondence drafting | Yes
| *Exam selection* | *No — governance first*
| Collections support | Yes
| Taxpayer service | Yes
|===

[.notes]
--
Walk the table top to bottom. The "yes" rows already have a human reviewer in
the loop, which makes them honest places to start.

Exam selection is deliberately excluded: that is a determination affecting a
taxpayer, and it needs a much heavier governance conversation than a drafting
assistant does. Saying this unprompted buys enormous credibility.
--

== Two rules of thumb

. *Start where a human already reviews the output.* If a person signs the letter
  today, a model that drafts it changes throughput without changing
  accountability.

. *Automate the toil around the model, not just the model.* Provisioning,
  patching, scaling and evidence collection are most of the work — and that
  problem is already solved.

*Automation* and *trust* run vertically through every layer.

[.notes]
--
Read bottom to top. The point of the picture is the two vertical bands:
automation and trust are not a layer you add at the end.

A project that treats compliance as a phase after deployment discovers, at the
worst possible moment, that it cannot produce evidence for anything that
already happened.

== The questions an AO will ask

[cols="2,2", options="header"]
|===
| Question | Control

| Where does our data go? | Inference runs on your cluster. Nowhere.
| Is this the approved model? | Signed artifacts, verified before admission
| Is the crypto validated? | FIPS mode, a supported operating state
| Still compliant next quarter? | Scheduled Compliance Operator scans
| Is it drifting? | TrustyAI drift and fairness on live traffic
| What if we are air-gapped? | Mirror images and models into the enclave
| Who is accountable? | A named reviewer. The model drafts; it never sends.
|===

[.notes]
--
This is the slide that unblocks the deal. Expect interruptions — let them
happen, this is the conversation you want.

Offer to take the whole table offline with their ISSO.
--

== Ninety days, then ninety more

[cols="1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Prove (0–90) | Ground (90–180) | Scale (180+)

| One endpoint on RHEL AI | Serving on OpenShift AI | Registry with real approvals
| Accelerator capacity plan | Retrieval over your guidance | Workflows two to five
| Data classes agreed in writing | Tune on agency vocabulary | Filing-season rehearsal
| One workflow with a reviewer | Drift and compliance wired | Extend to the enclave
| Baseline measured first | Provisioning automated | Report in mission terms
|===

[.notes]
--
Be honest about sequencing. Most agencies stall on rows 2 and 3 of the Prove
phase — no accelerator capacity plan, and no written agreement on which data
may be used. Neither is technical.

Ask the room directly which phase they are in.
--

Transition slide. Switch to a terminal now.

Tell them the labs run in simulate mode on a laptop with no cluster, and in

Run Lab 01 and stop hard on step 4 — the endpoint bound to 127.0.0.1.

== What happens after this hour

*Architecture workshop* — half a day with your platform and security teams.
Size accelerators, place the first workload, name the data classes in scope.

*Guided pilot* — one workflow, your infrastructure, a measured baseline and an
agreed definition of success before we start.

*Acquisition path* — Four Inc. and Carahsoft carry the vehicles. Bring them in
early, not at the end.

[.notes]
--
Close with a specific ask, not a thank-you. The architecture workshop is the
natural next step.

Then hand off for the contract vehicle conversation.
--

== One thing to remember

Nothing in these six labs required a public model endpoint, an internet
connection at inference time, or a rewrite of a system of record.

That is the whole argument.
